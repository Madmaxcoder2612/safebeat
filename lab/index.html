    How it works

    1. Find the beats. A simple detector finds heartbeats and measures the timing between them.
    2. Naive model. A small classifier says "regular" or "irregular" from that timing. It always answers.
    3. Signal-quality gate. Before answering, SafeBeat checks for a flat line, fast noise, uneven beat heights, beats that do not look alike, and an implausible beat count. The limits are learned from clean signals only.
    4. "I'm not sure" rule. Even on a good signal, SafeBeat says "can't tell" when the rhythm is too close to call (split conformal prediction, 90% target on clean data).

    Limits: please read

    • Every signal here is synthetic, generated by code written for this project. Real ECG is messier. Results on real recordings may be worse.
    • Damage strengths are chosen for teaching, not measured on real devices.
    • The 90% confidence promise holds only for clean signals like the calibration data. Damaged signals break that, which is why the gate comes first.
    • The gate assumes calibrated amplitude. A real device would need its own limits.
    • Baseline drift and clipping only trigger warnings here because they do not move beat timing for this simple detector. A different detector could need a different rule.
    • Two rhythm classes only, one lead, ten-second windows. This is not clinically validated and must not be used to make health decisions.
